# B.O.B. Roll-Off Passport Contract Review

**Prepared for:** Cassie Davis, representing Lucky Strike Entertainment Corporation  
**Proposed provider brand:** BowlVegas.com  
**Current contract provider:** Nexus Peak LLC, by and through Nathaniel Jones  
**Source reviewed:** Uploaded 11-page Event Management Services Agreement  
**Review status:** Business-term review and suggested revision outline; not legal advice

## Executive conclusion

The uploaded agreement provides a workable foundation for a single-event engagement, but it should not be sent for a new signature without correcting several material issues. The most important items are the incomplete fee table, inconsistent dates, unclear treatment of the Google Sheets integration, conflicting data-retention language, inconsistent cancellation language, and incomplete Client signature information.

The recommended approach is to preserve the core commercial model—**$249 setup and $1.99 per unique registered bowler**—while making the scope, data responsibilities, support limits, privacy terms, cancellation rules, and signature authority more precise. The revised document should identify Cassie Davis as Lucky Strike’s authorized representative and should identify the specific B.O.B. event and event dates before signature.

## 1. What the uploaded agreement currently says

The agreement is dated **June 9, 2026**. It names Nexus Peak LLC as Provider and Lucky Strike Entertainment Corporation, through Cassie Davis, as Client. It describes a single-event engagement that includes event configuration, claim codes, QR codes, Google Sheets integration, training, event-day support, post-event reporting, data archival, and a debrief consultation.

The requested commercial-brand change is from **Nexus Peak** to **BowlVegas.com**. A domain name is not automatically a legal entity. Until the BowlVegas.com name is formally registered as the contracting business or documented as a trade name of the legal entity, the safest signature block is **“Nexus Peak LLC, doing business as BowlVegas.com, by and through Nathaniel Jones.”** If another legal entity now owns the service, that entity should replace Nexus Peak LLC throughout the agreement and in the notice and signature sections.

The pricing section states a **$249.00 setup and consulting fee** plus a **$1.99 per registered bowler platform fee**. The total is described as `$249.00 + ($1.99 × number of bowlers)`. The Provider is to invoice within three business days after the event, and payment is due within 30 days. The agreement also states that work outside the listed scope is billed at $75 per hour.

The agreement includes platform features for bowlers, team captains, Event Directors, doorman check-in, Google Sheets integration, advertiser lead capture, mobile access, role-based permissions, claim codes, and analytics. It assigns Lucky Strike responsibility for providing accurate roster information, designating an Event Director and doorman staff, completing training, distributing claim codes, communicating material changes, and verifying data.

The agreement gives the Provider ownership of the platform and gives the Client ownership of its event data. It includes confidentiality language, a liability cap tied to fees paid for the specific event, a force-majeure and third-party-outage limitation, cancellation and postponement terms, termination rules, Nevada governing law, negotiation followed by mediation, and Clark County venue language.

The Provider’s signature appears completed, with Nathaniel Jones identified as Owner and a date of July 8, 2026. The Client signature area for Cassandra Davis is blank. The Appendix A service summary is dated July 9, 2026, which does not match the agreement date or signature date. Appendix B contains pricing examples for 25 through 200 bowlers.

## 2. Material issues to correct before sending to Cassie

### 2.1 Complete the fee table

The fee table appears to be present, but the contract text extracted from the uploaded PDF shows a blank or incomplete fee section before the later pricing clauses. The final agreement should state the fee formula in the body and in the pricing appendix using the same wording:

> **Setup fee:** $249.00 per event.  
> **Platform fee:** $1.99 per unique bowler who completes registration for the event.  
> **Total event fee:** $249.00 plus the platform fee multiplied by the final count of unique registered bowlers.

The agreement should state whether a guest, coordinator, staff member, captain, or duplicate registration is excluded from the bowler count. It should also state whether a bowler who registers and later cancels remains billable. The current language says cancelled registrations are not billed, but it does not define the cutoff or who determines whether a registration is cancelled.

### 2.2 Pricing strategy for BowlVegas.com

The original **$299 setup plus $1.99 per bowler** is easy to explain, but it underprices the work involved in roster preparation, QR and claim-code generation, portal configuration, training, offline check-in preparation, event-day support, troubleshooting, and post-event data handling. It also leaves little room for support time when an event has a large roster or multiple operational stations.

The strongest practical pricing model is a **standard price with a time-limited launch rate**, rather than permanently pricing the platform as a low-cost utility. The standard rate should communicate the value of a managed event system. The launch rate can reduce resistance for Lucky Strike’s first engagement while preserving a clear path to normal pricing.

| Pricing position | Setup fee | Per unique registered bowler | Approximate total for 902 bowlers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original idea | $299 | $1.99 | $2,093.98 |
| Approved Lucky Strike launch rate | $399 | $2.49 | $2,644.98 |
| Approved standard BowlVegas.com rate | $499 | $2.99 | $3,195.98 |
| High-touch / compressed timeline rate | $799 | $3.49 | $3,946.98 |

The approved offer is a **one-event launch rate of $399 plus $2.49 per unique registered bowler**, with the agreement stating that BowlVegas.com’s standard rate is **$499 plus $2.99 per unique registered bowler** for future events. The launch rate should expire after this event or after a defined short period. It should not become an indefinite discount.

The standard rate is still moderate compared with the operational value of a system that replaces manual claim-code preparation, supports multiple user portals, provides event-specific check-in controls, and includes technical preparation and event-day support. At 902 registered bowlers, the difference between the original idea and the recommended standard price is approximately $1,202. That additional revenue better compensates BowlVegas.com for development investment and high-volume operational risk without changing the pricing formula to something difficult for a client to understand.

The agreement should not charge the per-bowler fee for guests, coordinators, Event Director staff, doormen, duplicate accounts, or internal test records. A guest fee can be added later only if guest registration becomes a separately supported service. The contract should also define a minimum event charge, such as the setup fee, so a small event does not consume a full setup and support cycle for a very low total price.

The proposed launch rate should be presented as a **founding-client or first-event rate**, not as a claim that the system is unfinished. The sales explanation should focus on the work delivered: organized onboarding, personalized QR and claim-code materials, role-based portals, roster controls, check-in tools, offline contingency capability, and event-day support. No language should promise perfect uptime, guaranteed attendance, guaranteed revenue, or elimination of all manual work.

### 2.3 Identify the event and dates

The agreement should name the engagement as the **B.O.B. November 2026 event** and include the event date window, bowling date, and any banquet or pool-party dates that are within scope. If the dates are not final, the contract should say that they will be inserted by written amendment or in an attached event schedule.

The current document uses at least three dates: June 9, July 8, and July 9, 2026. The revised version should use one effective date and clearly label any later signature or appendix dates.

### 2.3 Confirm BowlVegas.com’s contracting identity

The revised agreement should use **BowlVegas.com** in the title, service descriptions, website references, and customer-facing materials. The legal party should remain the actual entity that owns and operates the service. Recommended wording:

> **SERVICE PROVIDER:** Nexus Peak LLC, a Nevada limited liability company doing business as BowlVegas.com, by and through Nathaniel Jones.

That wording should be changed if Nexus Peak LLC is not the current legal owner. The contract should not use “BowlVegas.com” alone as the legal party unless that name is registered and authorized to contract.

### 2.4 Clarify the Google Sheets integration

The current phrase “automatic creation of data to be synced to Client’s bowler roster” is too broad. The revised clause should state that the Client authorizes the Provider to use the designated Google Sheet and designated event tab for roster import and approved app-generated write-back fields.

The agreement should identify who controls Google account access, who grants and revokes access, and what happens if Google Sheets, an API, a network, or a permission setting is unavailable. The Provider should not be responsible for inaccurate source data supplied by the Client or for an outage caused by Google or another third party.

A recommended limitation is:

> The Client will provide a designated spreadsheet and event tab and will maintain the permissions necessary for the agreed import and write-back operations. The Provider may write only the app-managed fields and other fields expressly approved by the Client. The Client remains responsible for source-data accuracy and for reviewing imported and written-back data.

### 2.5 Resolve the data-retention conflict

Section 5.2 says the Client may request deletion within 30 days after event conclusion and that the Provider may archive data indefinitely after that period. Appendix A says “Data retention” is included and describes “indefinite access to Client’s event data.” These provisions should be replaced with one consistent rule.

A balanced option is to keep event data available to the Client for a defined period, such as 12 months after the event, unless the parties agree otherwise. After that period, the Provider may delete or anonymize the data, subject to legal, accounting, security, and backup-retention requirements. The agreement should distinguish live operational data from backup copies and anonymized aggregate analytics.

### 2.6 Make the cancellation rules internally consistent

The current agreement says the setup fee is refundable if the Client cancels before delivery of the initial roster, but it also says that once setup services have begun the $249 setup fee remains due. Those provisions can conflict if setup begins before the roster is delivered.

The suggested rule is to define a clear milestone:

> If the Client cancels before the Provider begins material setup work, the setup fee is refundable. Once the Provider begins material setup work, the setup fee is nonrefundable because it compensates for work already performed. No per-bowler platform fee is due for bowlers who never complete registration.

The revised agreement should use “material setup work” only if the parties define it or agree that the Provider’s written notice that setup has begun is sufficient.

### 2.7 Clarify service-day support and contingency limits

The contract promises “real-time technical support,” “live troubleshooting,” “real-time monitoring,” and “on-call availability for critical issues.” Those promises should identify the support window, contact method, expected response effort, and exclusions. Otherwise, the language may be read as an unlimited uptime or staffing commitment.

The suggested wording should state that the Provider will use commercially reasonable efforts to respond during the agreed event support window. It should also explain that the platform includes documented offline or backup procedures where available, but that the Provider does not guarantee uninterrupted service, internet connectivity, device compatibility, scanner hardware performance, or third-party service availability.

### 2.8 Correct the platform-feature description

The feature list should be reviewed against the version actually being delivered. The contract should not promise features that are still experimental, unavailable for the specific event, or dependent on a later implementation. The advertiser lead-capture item is especially important because it says the Client receives “15%” of ad space sold, but the agreement does not define the sales, attribution, payment, timing, or approval rules.

That item should either be removed from this event-services agreement or replaced with a separate sponsorship addendum. If retained, it should state that no advertising revenue, lead volume, sale, or payment is guaranteed and that any revenue share is governed by written terms agreed before a sale.

### 2.9 Strengthen privacy and security language

The current agreement includes general confidentiality and security language but does not clearly allocate responsibilities for personal information such as names, email addresses, phone numbers, age-related eligibility information, hotel data, roommate information, and QR or claim-code status.

The suggested contract should state that each party will use personal information only for event administration, registration, communication, check-in, safety, or other documented event purposes. It should require reasonable administrative, technical, and physical safeguards; prompt notice of a confirmed security incident affecting Client data; and cooperation in investigating and containing that incident.

The parties should also clarify that claim codes and QR codes are operational credentials, not public documents, and that the Client should distribute them only to the intended bowler or authorized recipient.

### 2.10 Revisit the liability and refund provisions

The current liability cap is the amount of fees paid for the specific event. It also excludes lost profits, data loss, and consequential damages. Section 6.4 states that if the platform becomes unavailable during an event due to Provider negligence, the Client may be entitled to a 100% refund of the $249.00 setup fee, but the sentence contains a grammatical error and does not define “unavailable.”

The clause should state whether the refund is the Client’s exclusive remedy for a qualifying service interruption. It should also consider carve-outs for obligations that are typically not capped, such as fraud, willful misconduct, confidentiality violations, or misuse of personal data. Those carve-outs are business decisions that should be reviewed by counsel before signing.

### 2.11 Complete the Client representative and notice information

The signature block identifies “Cassandra Davis,” while the project and account records identify **Cassie Davis**. The contract should use the legal name that appears in Cassie’s authority and Lucky Strike’s records. If Cassie is signing as an Event Director rather than as an officer or authorized contracting representative, the agreement should state that Lucky Strike has authorized her to sign.

The Client notice block should include the correct business address, email, and phone number. The Provider’s contact block should likewise be confirmed before the final document is circulated.

## 3. Recommended suggested-contract position

The suggested version should retain the current commercial concept but use the following structure:

1. **Parties and authority.** Identify the legal Provider as the entity doing business as BowlVegas.com and identify Lucky Strike Entertainment Corporation as Client. Identify Cassie Davis as the authorized representative for the event, subject to confirmation of her signing authority.
2. **Event schedule.** Name the B.O.B. November 2026 event and list the event dates, bowling date, support window, and any included banquet or pool-party operations.
3. **Included services.** Describe setup, roster import, app-managed write-back, claim codes, QR materials, training, check-in support, documentation, and post-event export using only features confirmed for delivery.
4. **Client responsibilities.** State roster deadlines, source-data accuracy, access permissions, staff availability, training participation, claim-code distribution, and timely issue reporting.
5. **Fees.** State the agreed launch or standard setup fee and per-bowler fee. For the approved Lucky Strike launch rate, use $399.00 plus $2.49 per unique registered bowler. Define duplicates, cancelled registrations, guests, and the final-count reconciliation process.
6. **Support and contingencies.** Define event support hours, response efforts, third-party outage limitations, hardware and network responsibilities, and the available offline or paper fallback process.
7. **Data, privacy, and security.** Define ownership, permitted uses, access controls, incident notice, retention, deletion, anonymization, and the Google Sheet boundary.
8. **Intellectual property.** Preserve Provider ownership of the platform and grant Lucky Strike a limited right to use the configured event materials and export its Client data.
9. **Cancellation, postponement, and termination.** Use one setup-fee milestone, define refunds, carryover treatment for postponement, and provide delivery of Client data after termination.
10. **Liability and dispute terms.** Confirm the cap, exclusions, any carve-outs, Nevada law, negotiation, mediation, venue, and attorney-fee treatment.
11. **General terms and signatures.** Add amendment, notice, assignment, severability, entire-agreement, electronic-signature, and authority language.
12. **Appendices.** Attach one event schedule, one fee schedule, and—if needed—a separate advertising or sponsorship addendum.

## 4. Decisions needed from the parties

Before a clean signature-ready revision is prepared, the parties should confirm the following points:

- Is the contracting Client **Lucky Strike Entertainment Corporation**, or another Lucky Strike legal entity?
- Is Cassie Davis authorized to sign for Lucky Strike, and should the contract use “Cassie” or her legal name “Cassandra”?
- What are the exact B.O.B. November 2026 event dates and which event operations are included?
- Should the $249 setup fee become nonrefundable when material setup begins, or only after the initial roster is delivered?
- Should the contract reflect the approved one-event launch rate of $399 plus $2.49 per unique registered bowler, with the standard BowlVegas.com rate of $499 plus $2.99 applying afterward?
- Is the per-bowler fee based on each unique bowler who completes registration, including a bowler who later cancels?
- What data-retention period should apply after the event?
- Should advertising lead capture and the 15% reference be removed from this agreement and placed in a separate addendum?
- What support hours and contact channels are being promised on event day?
- What refund, credit, or other remedy should apply to a Provider-caused outage?
- What email and physical notice addresses should appear for each party?

## Recommended next step

Treat the uploaded document as the **current/original agreement** and do not circulate it as the final version until the issues above are resolved. The strongest next draft would use BowlVegas.com as the public-facing brand, identify the correct legal Provider, offer Lucky Strike the approved one-event launch rate of $399.00 plus $2.49 per unique registered bowler, state the future standard rate of $499.00 plus $2.99, remove the 15% advertising revenue language in favor of a separate addendum, reconcile the data-retention and cancellation provisions, and add a precise event schedule and privacy/security section.

This review is a business drafting aid, not legal advice. The parties should have a qualified attorney review the final agreement before signature, particularly the liability, privacy, payment, indemnity, and dispute provisions.
